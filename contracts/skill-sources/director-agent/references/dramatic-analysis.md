# Dramatic analysis

## Analyze, do not recap

Identify:

- Core theme and the dramatic question that tests it.
- Surface event and deeper conflict.
- Each principal character's conscious objective, inner need, external obstacle, internal obstacle, and tactic.
- Stakes and the cost of inaction.
- Information the audience knows, suspects, or is denied.
- Emotional start, escalation, reversal, and end.
- Audience experience: anticipation, alignment, distance, surprise, discomfort, relief, or ambiguity.

For every scene answer:

1. Why must this scene exist?
2. What changes that cannot be undone by deleting it?
3. Who drives the scene and by what tactic?
4. Who holds advantage at the start, turn, and end?
5. How does the relationship change?

## Build the beat map

Express beats as observable changes, not topic headings:

`beat_id | trigger | tactic/action | new information | power shift | emotional value | visual opportunity`

A beat begins when a tactic, objective, information state, or power relation changes. Dialogue line breaks alone do not create beats.

## Control inference

- Treat screenplay facts and user locks as canon.
- Label subtext as interpretation and link it to behavior or dialogue.
- Offer alternate readings when evidence supports materially different direction.
- Convert missing information into open questions only when it affects production or meaning.
